# Source-type checklists

**Bottom line:** go through every row of the checklist that fits the job, and mark each one Found, Searched with none found, or Not applicable (see `research-method.md`, Step 2). The lists are a floor, not a ceiling: add any new type you find by following links.

Which checklist:

- **A. Companies and organisations:** a B2B client (software or services). The usual case.
- **B. Local and consumer customers:** a handoff file with `client_type` `local_consumer`.
- **C. One person's own words:** researching an expert, a founder or a buyer before outreach, and the review-panel research. Also the benchmark test for this method.

---

## A. Companies and organisations

**Official and complete lists (start here: they define the market)**

- Company registry (in Nigeria, the Corporate Affairs Commission)
- Sector regulator and licence registers, and their notices (new licences, upgrades, revocations, sanctions)
- Official gazettes; court and insolvency notices
- Government programme lists (grant recipients, approved vendors, accredited providers)
- Public tenders and procurement portals: buyers and bidders

**Member and attendee lists**

- Trade and industry associations: member directories
- Chambers of commerce
- Professional bodies (for firms of accountants, lawyers, doctors and so on)
- Conference speaker, sponsor and exhibitor lists
- Award shortlists and "top 50" lists in the trade press

**Commercial databases**

- B2B databases (Apollo, Prospeo, and others the user has). Plan limits are recorded in the search-query skill. This skill names the database and the filters to try; it never writes a search link from memory
- Aggregator and scraped directories (business-listing sites). Flag them: coverage and accuracy are unknown until a sample is checked
- Maps listings (Google Maps and local equivalents): locations, categories, reviews

**Signals that the pain is present (from the handoff's `signals[].where`)**

- Job boards and careers pages: roles, tools named, how much hiring
- Review sites and maps reviews: complaints, new locations
- App stores: whether an app exists, its rating, recent complaint reviews
- Changes on the company's own site: a new branch, product or pricing page
- Press, funding and partnership announcements
- Partner and integration directories of the platforms the client works with
- Technology look-ups (which tools a site runs), where the ICP has a technographic filter

**Communities**

- LinkedIn groups and company pages
- Industry WhatsApp, Telegram or Slack groups (usually private: list as "ask to join", never as searched)
- Forums and subreddits for the role or the sector

---

## B. Local and consumer customers

- Ad-platform audience tools (Meta, Google, TikTok): audience size estimates for the catchment and interests. Record the date and the exact settings
- Population and household figures for the catchment (census, statistics office, local government)
- Maps listings of places where Tier 1 people gather (estates, offices, schools, other businesses)
- Local partners: businesses that serve the same people
- Community groups: estate associations, parents' groups, running clubs, church and mosque groups
- Local events and markets
- Local press and city guides
- Review sites for competitors: what their customers complain about

---

## C. One person's own words

- Their own website and blog: the archive or sitemap, every page
- Newsletter (Substack, Beehiiv, Ghost and similar): the archive page and every page of it, including video posts
- Other newsletters they write for, or guest posts
- LinkedIn: their activity and posts, then a search of posts by their name and handle
- X (Twitter), Threads, Bluesky: their profile; mirror sites when the profile will not open
- YouTube and video: their own channel, and talks on event channels
- Podcasts: their own show's episode list, then guest appearances (search the name with "podcast", "episode", "interview", "guest")
- Conference talks and webinars: speaker pages, slides, recordings
- Books, courses, templates and product pages they wrote
- Interviews and profiles about them (third party: lower weight, but they point to primary sources)
- GitHub and other public work
- Communities they post in (forums, Reddit, Slack communities with public archives)

Rank their own words first; third-party descriptions are leads to primary sources, not evidence of what they said.
