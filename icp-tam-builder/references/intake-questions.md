# Intake questions and client request

Contents:
- Part 0: Inputs checklist (show this to the user first)
- Part A: Client request message (send this to the client)
- Part B: Questions to ask the user
- Part C: Customer conversations (5-minute kit and deeper interview guide)
- Part D: Rules for asking

---

## Part 0: Inputs checklist (show this to the user first)

Most users know to send the website and nothing else. Before a Full build (or a Refresh) does any work, show them everything that would improve the result, why each item matters, and what they have already given. Then wait.

**How to build it**

- Pick the list for the client type: the core list (B2B software and services), the local / consumer list, or the public-only list when the user has no access to the client (portfolio work, pre-sales, a client who has not engaged yet).
- Mark every item **Have**, **Missing** or **Not applicable**, using only the conversation and any files given. Do not crawl the website yet; a URL counts as Have.
- Keep the "What it changes" column. It is what tells the user which missing items are worth chasing.
- Put the blocking questions (Part B) in the same message, with defaults.
- End by saying what happens if they go ahead now: the evidence tier they will get, and which outputs will rest on `[Assumption]`.
- Offer the client request message (Part A) if the missing items have to come from the client: A1 for a business that sells to businesses, A2 for a local or consumer business.
- Show the checklist once per job. If the user's first message already says to start now, put it at the top as "what would improve this later" and carry on.

### Core list: B2B software and services

**Must have** (the work can start with these alone, at Hypothesis tier)

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Website URL, plus any other selling pages (LinkedIn page, directory listings, landing pages) | Shows who the client *says* it sells to; the starting point for everything |
| 2 | The one offer this ICP is for | One product or service per ICP; mixing offers blurs the profile |
| 3 | Price or typical deal size, and the pricing model (per seat, retainer, project, usage) | Without it, every TAM / SAM / SOM figure is an `[Assumption]` |
| 4 | Where the client can serve customers (countries, regions, languages, regulatory limits) | Sets the SAM; stops the market being sized globally by mistake |

**Biggest jump in quality** (any one of these moves the work from Hypothesis to Grounded)

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Sales call recordings or transcripts, won and lost: at least 5 to start; where recordings of won deals exist, ideally 20 to 30 of them | Real pains, triggers, objections and the buyer's own words for messaging. Pain heuristics resting on 5 won calls are a start; 20 to 30 won accounts make them far more trustworthy |
| 6 | Customer list or CRM export: company, industry, employees, location, deal size, date won, lead source, status (active / churned / open deal), buyer's job title | Shows who actually buys; allows win rate, deal size and churn by segment, and the winners-versus-losers test that sets the scoring weights |
| 7 | The client's five best customers, and whether a 5-minute call with each is possible | The fastest route to Grounded when there are no transcripts (Part C1) |

**Needed for a real SOM** (otherwise it is assumed)

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Sales capacity: number of salespeople, meetings each per month, close rate, length of the sales cycle | Turns SOM from a guess into a capacity calculation |
| 9 | Services only: delivery hours available a year, hours each client takes a year, current number of clients | Caps SOM at what the team can actually deliver |

**Very helpful**

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Reasons for lost deals, and the main competitors (including "do nothing") | Builds the negative ICP and disqualifiers, and the list of real alternatives in Step 4 ("Why they buy") |
| 11 | Case studies, testimonials, survey or NPS results that are not on the website | More evidence per segment; confirms or overturns the website's claims |
| 12 | Sales deck, proposal template, pricing sheet | Confirms the offer, price and the language the client already uses |
| 13 | A list of target companies to rank (CSV), if one exists | Lets the scoring model rank a real list straight away |

**If they exist**

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Earlier ICP, persona or market-research documents, and any market-size figures the client already quotes | Shows what changed, and gives a top-down cross-check |
| 15 | Ad, email or website analytics showing which audiences respond | Behavioural evidence for the readiness score and the channels |
| 16 | Sectors, regions or company types the client refuses to serve | Disqualifiers that no data source would reveal |

### Local business / consumer list

Use items 1 to 4 (with "catchment" in place of "where the client can serve") and items 10, 12, 14 and 15, then replace items 5 to 9, 11, 13 and 16 with:

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Links to, or an export of, Google and social reviews | Who the customers are and what they value, in their words |
| L2 | Booking, till (POS) or loyalty data with personal details removed: visits, average spend, repeat rate | The real best-customer group and the annual spend per customer |
| L3 | Five short conversations with regulars (Part C1) | Moves the work to Grounded |
| L4 | Where customers come from: neighbourhoods, postcodes, delivery radius or travel time | Defines the catchment, which sets the SAM |
| L5 | Physical capacity: seats, slots or covers per day, days open, typical utilisation | Caps SOM at what the premises can serve |

### Public-only list (no access to the client)

The result will stay **Hypothesis tier** and an **Early Customer Profile**; deal size, CRM patterns and capacity will all be `[Assumption]`. These are what the user can still gather, in order of value:

| # | Item | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Website URL (case studies, customers, pricing, careers and "vs competitor" pages are read in Step 1) | The client's claims and its published proof |
| P2 | Review-site pages (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Google), pasted if the page blocks automated reading | Third-party buyer language, reviewers' job titles and company sizes |
| P3 | The company's LinkedIn page: staff count, recent hires, posts | Size, growth and which teams are hiring |
| P4 | Recent job posts by the client and by its customers | Tools in use, team structure and priorities |
| P5 | Press, funding announcements, podcast or webinar transcripts where founders or customers speak | Triggers, positioning and unscripted customer language |
| P6 | A real company count from a database or register search with the ICP filters (Clay, Sales Navigator, Apollo, a company register) | The one input that turns a derived count into a counted one, and the biggest single improvement to the TAM |
| P7 | Any public pricing, including third-party listings | Replaces the price assumption, or at least gives it a source |

### Message format

```
Before I start, here is everything that would make this ICP and market size better.
I've marked what you have already given me.

| # | Item | What it changes | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Website URL | ... | Have |
| 2 | ... | ... | Missing |

Send whatever you can (files, links or pasted text); partial is fine.
Or reply "go with what I have" and I'll start now. You'll get a [tier] result, and
[the items that will be assumptions] will be marked [Assumption].

Need to ask the client for these? I can draft the request message for you
([business-to-business / local and consumer] version).

Before I start, [up to five blocking questions, each with a default]

Optional: in one or two lines, who do you think buys from this client, and why?
```

## Part A: Client request message

Pick the version for the client type from Step 0: **A1** for a business that sells to businesses (software or services), **A2** for a local or consumer business. Never send A1 to a salon, clinic or restaurant.

Before sending:
- Fill in every [bracket]. The data-handling line is **required**: do not send the message with it blank.
- Leave out anything you already have. Never ask the client for their own website.
- Keep the two levels: the 10-minute minimum, then everything else marked optional. A small client who is shown nine required items sends nothing.

### A1. Businesses that sell to businesses

> Hi [name],
>
> By [date] I'll send you a one-page view of who your best customers are, which companies to go after first, and roughly how many new customers you could win this year. To base it on your business rather than guesswork, could you send me what you can from the list below?
>
> **If you only have 10 minutes,** send one of these:
> - a list of your current customers, with a rough size for each and what each pays you, and a note of your five best; or
> - a time for a 20-minute call instead, where I ask the questions; or
> - recordings of 5 recent sales calls, if you record them.
>
> **Everything else is optional.** In order of how much it helps:
> 1. More recordings or transcripts of sales calls, won and lost. If you have recordings of deals you won, 20 to 30 of them is ideal, but any number helps.
> 2. A customer list or CRM export with whatever you have of: company name, industry, number of staff, location, deal size, date won, how they found you, whether they are still a customer, any open deals, and the job title of the person who bought.
> 3. An introduction to your five best customers. I would ask each of them these five questions, about five minutes in all:
>    1. What problem were you trying to solve when you first looked for a solution like this?
>    2. What else did you try or consider before choosing [client], and why did you pick [client] over it?
>    3. What almost stopped you from buying?
>    4. What result have you seen since?
>    5. If a peer in your industry had the same problem, how would you describe [client] to them?
>
>    I'll send you the script first, and I won't sell to them or ask them for anything else. If you'd rather, you can ask them yourself and forward me the answers.
> 4. Your main competitors, and the reasons you've lost deals.
> 5. Any customer survey or feedback results, and case studies or testimonials that aren't on your website.
> 6. Your sales deck, proposal template and price list.
> 7. Any earlier customer research, and any ad, email or website figures showing which audiences respond best.
>
> **How I'll handle what you send:** [where I'll store it, whether an AI tool processes it, who sees it, and when I'll delete it]. Please remove personal details I don't need (private phone numbers, emails, ID numbers), and only send material you have the right to share.

### A2. Local and consumer businesses

> Hi [name],
>
> By [date] I'll send you a one-page view of who your best customers are, where they come from, and how many more you could realistically serve this year. Could you send me what you can from the list below?
>
> **If you only have 10 minutes,** send one of these:
> - a rough picture of your regulars: about how many, how often they come, what they usually spend, and which areas they come from; or
> - a time for a 20-minute call instead, where I ask the questions.
>
> **Everything else is optional.** In order of how much it helps:
> 1. Links to your Google and social media reviews.
> 2. Booking, till or loyalty figures with names and contact details removed: visits, average spend, how often people come back.
> 3. Where your customers come from: neighbourhoods, postcodes, delivery radius or travel time.
> 4. How many people you can serve: seats, slots or covers a day, days open, and how full you usually are.
> 5. A chance to ask five of your regulars five short questions, about five minutes in all:
>    1. What were you looking for when you first came to [client]?
>    2. Where else did you go or consider, and why did you choose [client]?
>    3. What nearly put you off?
>    4. What has changed for you since?
>    5. How would you describe [client] to a friend?
>
>    I'll send you the script first. If you'd rather, you can ask them yourself and forward me the answers.
> 6. The other places your customers compare you with.
> 7. Your prices or menu, and anything you've noticed about which offers or adverts bring people in.
>
> **How I'll handle what you send:** [where I'll store it, whether an AI tool processes it, who sees it, and when I'll delete it]. Please remove names and contact details from anything you send, and only send material you have the right to share.

**Data-protection note for the user:** confirm the client has the right to share customer calls and data with you, and strip personal contact details that the analysis does not need. The handling line must say what you will actually do. Whether that wording is adequate depends on the data-protection law that applies to you and the client; this template is not legal advice, so have it checked where that matters.

## Part B: Questions to ask the user

### Blocking questions

Ask these if the answer is not already in the conversation or the files. Offer a default so the user can reply "yes" or pick one.

1. **Which offer is this ICP for?** One product or service per ICP. (Default: the offer with the most customer proof on the website, otherwise the most prominent one. Say in one line why it was chosen over the others; each other offer gets its own ICP later.)
2. **Who pays the client: a business for software, a business for a service, or consumers?** This picks the method (see `client-types.md`). For consumers, switch from firmographics to a demographic, behavioural and catchment-based persona and say so. (Default: infer from the website, then confirm.)
3. **Where can the client actually serve customers?** Country, regions, languages, regulatory limits. (Default: infer from the website, then confirm.)
4. **What is the average annual contract value (ACV) or deal size, and what is the pricing model?** Needed for the bottom-up TAM. (If unknown, use a range from the website's pricing page and mark it `[Assumption]`.)
5. **What will the ICP be used for?** Outbound list-building, messaging and copy, positioning, product decisions, or pitching the client? This decides which use packs are produced (see "Delivery format" in `output-template.md`); the client-facing summary is always produced. (Default: outbound list-building, so all packs.)
6. **What evidence exists?** Transcripts, CRM export, surveys, customer access. This sets the evidence tier. The inputs checklist's Have / Missing column usually answers it, so ask it only when the checklist leaves it unclear. That keeps the questions asked to five or fewer.

**Optional, and not counted in the five: your own view.** "In one or two lines, who do you think buys from this client, and why?" Ask it every time and say it is optional. If the user answers, record it as the operator's hypothesis, before any analysis, and show in the brief's summary where the evidence agrees with it and where it does not. It gives the user a baseline to judge the output against, instead of having the thinking done for them.

### Probing questions (after reading the site and evidence)

Pick only the ones that fill a real gap.

**Who buys**
- Which customers are the *best*, and by what measure: revenue, retention, easiest sale, referrals?
- Who signs the contract versus who pushes for it internally versus who can block it?
- Which customers were a mistake? Why?
- Who should definitely *not* buy this?

**Why they buy**
- What was happening in the customer's business when they decided to buy? (Trigger events)
- What were they doing before: spreadsheets, a competitor, an agency, nothing?
- What can the client do that those alternatives can't, and which customers care most about that?
- What nearly stopped the deal?
- What result do customers actually get, and how soon?

**Why they don't**
- Top reasons for lost deals?
- Which competitors or alternatives come up most, including "do nothing"?

**Reconciling conflicts** (use when the evidence disagrees)
- The website says [X] but the customer evidence points to [Y]. Which is the intended market and which is where the revenue actually comes from?

**How customers buy** (sets how much evidence you need)
- Do customers buy self-serve, after a short call, through a multi-stage sale, or through field or named-account selling?
- How many comparable best-fit customers does the client have today?

**Market size and capacity** (needed for SOM)
- How many salespeople or SDRs, how many meetings each per month, and what close rate and sales cycle?
- Any market-size figures or industry reports the client already uses?
- Sectors, regions or company types the client refuses to serve?

## Part C: Customer conversations

### C1. Top-5 customer conversation kit

Use when there are no transcripts, surveys or interviews. Five customers x five minutes is the fastest way to move from Hypothesis to Grounded.

**Pick the five:** the client's best customers by a mix of revenue, retention and satisfaction. Avoid choosing only the friendliest ones.

**How to ask:** a 5-minute call or voice note. Ask the questions in this order and let them talk. Record the call (with permission) so it can be analysed afterwards.

1. What problem were you trying to solve when you first looked for a solution like this?
2. What else did you try or consider before choosing us, and why did you pick us over it?
3. What almost stopped you from buying?
4. What result have you seen since?
5. If a peer in your industry had the same problem, how would you describe us to them?

Question 5 is the most valuable: it returns the buyer's own language and who they think "someone like me" is.

**Also record, without adding a sixth question:** did the person on the call push for the purchase inside their company, or did someone else (who)? If it has not come up by the end, ask it in one line. This names the champion.

When the user returns with the answers, treat them as transcripts in Step 2. The client request message (Part A) contains these five questions, so the client can see exactly what their customers will be asked.

### C2. Deeper interview guide (30 to 45 minutes)

Use when the client can give access to customers. Aim for 5 to 8 interviews to surface themes, and 12 to 20 per persona segment to get close to saturation. Do not turn a theme into a rule until broader account data supports it.

**Who to interview:** a mix, not just the happiest customers. Include best-fit customers, customers who struggled or churned, and where possible people who evaluated the client and chose someone else.

**Before the call:** pull deal notes, onboarding history and any recordings, and write a one-page account overview. Use the same guide each time so answers can be compared.

**Open:** thank them, explain it is not a sales call, confirm the end time, ask permission to record.

**Questions, in order:**

1. *Situation:* Tell me about your role and what your team does. How big is the business and what pressure is it under? What do you run today?
2. *Pain:* Before you chose this, what was the biggest problem? What was it costing you, in time, money or stress? What had you already tried?
3. *Trigger:* What changed that made you start looking? Was there a specific moment?
4. *Alternatives:* What else did you consider, including doing nothing? Why did you choose this over them?
5. *Decision:* Who evaluated, who championed, who could have blocked it, who signed? What decided it in the end? What nearly stopped it?
6. *Implementation:* How did the rollout go? Who was involved? What surprised you?
7. *Impact:* What result have you seen? How would you measure it? What happens if you stopped?
8. *Success criteria:* How did you define success before buying, and did it turn out to be the right measure?
9. *Language:* If you described this to a peer in one sentence, what would you say?

**Persona questions to add when building personas:** typical day and top goals; how they are measured; who else is involved; must-have versus nice-to-have; common red flags; where they look for information and who they trust.

**After the call:** transcribe, then extract (a) the five to ten most quotable lines with context, (b) a short summary under each heading above, (c) attributes of the company for the evidence table. Keep quotes verbatim and attributed to the interview ID. Ask permission before using any quote publicly.

## Part D: Rules for asking

- Ask in one grouped round of five questions or fewer. Do not drip questions one at a time. The optional "your own view" question (Part B) is extra and is always marked optional.
- The inputs checklist (Part 0) and the blocking questions go in one message. That message is the only pause before work starts.
- The second and last pause in a Full build is the ICP sign-off (SKILL.md Step 6). The gap and contradiction questions from Step 3 wait for it, each with its default; do not send them on their own.
- Do not ask what the website, files or conversation already answer.
- Give a default answer with each question so the user can confirm quickly.
- Prefer tappable multiple-choice when the interface supports it; otherwise number the questions.
- If the user says "I don't know", record the item as `[Assumption]`, move on, and add it to the validation plan.
- When the user's answers contradict the evidence, say so plainly and ask which to trust. Do not quietly pick one.
